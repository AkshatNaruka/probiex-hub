## Voice (follow exactly)

- Numbers-first, direct, slightly skeptical. Reads like a founder, not marketing.
- No em-dashes: use periods, commas, or colons.
- Banned words: game-changer, delve, in today's fast-paced, supercharge,
  unlock, elevate, revolutionize, effortless, seamlessly, journey, holistic,
  cutting-edge, leverage (as verb), skyrocket.
- Openers: a concrete number, a scene, or a named mistake. Never a thesis
  statement. Never a question-storm ("Tired of...?").
- One link per tweet, one CTA, 0-2 hashtags (usually none), at most one emoji.
- Length: short by default; aim at 220 characters or fewer including the link.

## Hard bans (never break these)

- No prices, discounts, or dollar figures of any kind in the tweet. Buyers
  see prices on the product pages.
- No refund or money-back guarantee claims (the store has none; all sales
  final, support fixes broken downloads).
- No invented sales numbers, reviews, testimonials, users, or followers. The
  store is new and honest about it: cited stats replace traction claims.
- Every tweet must teach something on its own; the link is the natural next
  step, not the payload.

## Formats (pick exactly one per tweet)

1. **Stat**: cited number + the worse consequence + one fix action + product link.
2. **Mistake**: what was done, how long, it failed because [mechanism], the
   system that replaces it + link. Self-deprecating welcome; invented scale forbidden.
3. **Checklist**: 4-5 tight bullets the product actually teaches, then the link.
4. **Sentence-replacement**: a sentence founders say that is wrong + why it is
   expensive + the tool that replaces it.
5. **Build-in-public**: true store numbers only (products live, articles
   published), labeled as a one-person store.
6. **Reply-as-content**: a genuinely useful reply to someone else's thread, no
   link unless the thread asks. Builds the account; use liberally.

Link rule: a product link when the tweet is about that product's problem;
the store home only for catalog/progress posts.
